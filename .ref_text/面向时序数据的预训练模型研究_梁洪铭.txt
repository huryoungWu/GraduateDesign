Abstract
With the rapid development of the IoT, industrial monitoring, and intelligent sensing technologies, time series data have experienced explosive growth in industrial, medical, and financial fields. As a core technology for data-driven decision-making, time series analysis supports downstream tasks such as forecasting, classification, and anomaly detection. However, traditional statistical models and deep learning approaches face challenges in computational efficiency and insufficient modeling of local features when handling long sequences and multimodal data. Recent successes of pre-trained models in natural language processing have inspired new directions for time series analysis, but existing temporal pre-training frameworks suffer from large parameter counts and weak modeling of local temporal dependencies.
To address these limitations, this study proposes a lightweight pre-trained framework based on the state-space model Mamba, incorporating bidirectional strategies and frequency-domain enhancement techniques. This framework aims to enhance the model’s generalization capability for temporal patterns and sensitivity to local features. The main contributions include:
(1) Constructing a time series pre-trained model named Large Pre-trained Linear Mamba (LPLM) by integrating linear layers with the Mamba architecture. As a state-space model, Mamba achieves linear-complexity forward propagation through selective scanning algorithms, significantly reducing computational costs. To address local temporal dependencies, we propose a time-decay weighted loss function and a global sequence linear module that dynamically emphasizes recent time-point features while extracting both global and local characteristics. During pre-training, the model learns domain-agnostic temporal knowledge by reconstructing randomly masked time series patches. Experiments demonstrate that LPLM achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art models on forecasting, classification, and anomaly detection tasks. Compared to the baseline MOMENT model, LPLM reduces parameters by 75.55% (24.45% of the original) and training time by 33.75% (66.25% of the original), validating its computational efficiency.
(2) Introducing a bidirectional strategy to overcome the unidirectional information flow limitation of Mamba. By constructing bidirectional Mamba modules that jointly
